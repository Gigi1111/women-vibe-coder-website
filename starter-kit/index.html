01

Before you start

You need
  • A laptop or computer (a phone works for reading, but building is easier on a bigger screen)
  • Internet and a web browser, such as Chrome, Safari, Edge or Firefox
  • An email address, to sign up for the free tools
  • About 30 minutes, and a warm drink ☕
You don't need
  • Any coding experience
  • To install anything
  • To pay for anything to get started
  • To understand every word you see. Skip what doesn't make sense yet.

How to use this kit: do Kit 1 first if you like guided lessons, or jump to Kit 2 if you'd rather just try building something today. Kit 3 is for reading whenever you get stuck.

02

Words you'll see

You don't need to memorise these. Come back whenever a word trips you up.

AI tool / AI assistant
A website you chat with. You type a message, it types back, and some can build things for you.
Prompt
The message you type to the AI. Writing a clear prompt is the main skill of vibe coding.
Vibe coding
Building software by describing what you want in everyday words, instead of writing code yourself.
App / tool / prototype
The thing you build: a small webpage, a checklist, a game. "Prototype" just means a first, rough version.
Preview
The window where the AI tool shows you what it built, so you can click around and try it.
GitHub
A website where people share projects and guides for free. Two of our kits live there. You can read it without an account.
Repository ("repo")
One project's page on GitHub. Think of it as a shared folder.
README
The main text of a GitHub project, shown below its list of files. This is the part you read.
Fork
A saved copy of someone's GitHub project. We forked our three kits so you'll always have a copy, even if the originals change.
03

How to read a GitHub page

Two of the kits are on GitHub. The page can look busy and technical at first, but you only need one part of it.

  1. Click the link. No account or sign-in is needed. If GitHub asks you to sign up, close that box or scroll past it.
  2. Ignore the list of files at the top. Those are for people who want to work on the project. You don't need them.
  3. Scroll down until you see large text and headings. That's the README, the actual guide.
  4. Look for "Contents" or "Table of Contents". Every item in it is a link: click one to jump to that part.
  5. Use your browser's Back button (←) to return to where you were.
Tip

If a page is in a language you'd rather not read, Chrome and Safari can translate it: right-click anywhere on the page and choose Translate.

The Easy-Vibe course: learn step by step

Guided lessons10 languages incl. EnglishStage 1 ≈ 4 hours, split as you like

A free, friendly course with pictures and interactive demos, used by tens of thousands of learners. In the very first chapter you build a small game in your browser just by chatting with AI.

  1. Open the course in English: datawhalechina.github.io/easy-vibe/en/ If the page shows another language, click the language name in the top menu and choose English.
  2. Read "How to Learn This Course" first. It's a short introduction to what vibe coding is and where the course goes. …/easy-vibe/en/stage-1/learning-map/
  3. Then do "Primary 1: AI Era, If You Can Speak, You Can Code." This is the hands-on chapter where you build a Snake game. …/easy-vibe/en/stage-1/ai-capabilities-through-games/
  4. Sign up for the AI tool the chapter uses. The lesson builds on a website called z.ai, which you'll need to sign up for with your email. The course also lists Google AI Studio (aistudio.google.com/apps) as a recommended alternative if you already have a Google account.
  5. Follow along at your own pace. Copy the course's example messages into the AI tool, look at what it builds, then change one small thing. Stop whenever you like; the page will be there tomorrow.
  6. Stop after Stage 1. Stages 2 and 3 go into more technical topics like databases and deployment. They're great for later, but not needed to get started.
Remember

As the course itself says: completion is more important than perfection. A wobbly game you finished beats a perfect one you never started.

05 · Kit 2
2

Awesome Vibe Coding: pick your first tool

A list of toolsOn GitHub10 minutes to choose

A popular, well-kept list of AI building tools. You don't read it top to bottom. Use it like a menu: pick one tool and try it.

  1. Open the list: github.com/Gigi1111/awesome-vibe-coding Scroll down past the file list (see How to read a GitHub page).
  2. In "Contents", click "Browser-based Tools". These run inside your web browser, so there's nothing to install.
  3. Pick one tool. If you can't decide, try one of these three from the list. Each lets you describe an app in plain words and shows you a live preview:
    • Lovable (lovable.dev)
    • Bolt.new (bolt.new)
    • Replit (replit.com)
  4. Sign up with your email. Most of these let you try a number of messages for free. Free limits change often, so check the tool's pricing page, and don't enter card details unless you've decided you want to pay.
  5. Skip the other sections for now. "IDEs", "Plugins", "Command Line Tools" and "Local Apps" are for people who already code.

Your first 15-minute project

  1. In the tool you chose, find the big text box (it often says something like "What do you want to build?").
  2. Paste your first prompt from this kit, or describe a small thing you wish existed in your daily life.
  3. Press Enter and wait about a minute. Watch the preview appear.
  4. Click around your new app. What do you like? What feels off?
  5. Ask for one change, e.g. "Make the buttons bigger and the colours softer."
  6. Done! Take a screenshot and share it in our Discord. Every tiny build counts. 🎉

The short version, in plain words

A good prompt has 4 parts
  1. Context: what you're making and who it's for
  2. Task: the one thing you want done right now
  3. Limits: what it should or shouldn't do
  4. What you want back: what success looks like
Context: I'm making a simple webpage for my book club of 8 friends. Task: Make a page that lists the book we're reading this month and the date of our next meeting. Limits: Keep it to one page, with big readable text and soft colours. No sign-up or login. What I want back: A finished page I can see in the preview.
When it breaks

Tell the AI exactly what you did, what you expected, and what happened instead. If there's an error message, copy all of it into the chat.

Stuck in a loop?

If every fix breaks something else, stop. Go back to the last version that worked and describe the problem fresh in a new chat.

It changed too much?

Say: "Only change the button colour. Don't touch anything else."

Sounds sure, but wrong?

AI can explain confidently and still be mistaken. Click and test it yourself rather than trusting the explanation.

Summary adapted from How to Start Vibe Coding by ai-for-developers, shared under CC0 (public domain): github.com/ai-for-developers/how-to-vibe-code.

Prompts to copy

Copy any of these into your AI tool as they are, or change the details to fit your life.

Your first build

Make me a calm, mobile-friendly checklist where I can tap three breakfast ideas for tomorrow morning. Use soft colours and big buttons.
Build a cozy reading tracker where I can log a few pages before bed and see a gentle progress bar for my five favourite books.
Create a simple page that shows a different kind, encouraging message each time I press a button.

Gentle helpers while you build

Explain this in plain English before changing anything.
Make the buttons larger and calmer to read.
Walk me through the simplest way to share this with a friend.
I'm a complete beginner. Ask me 3 questions about what I want before you start building.
Only change the colour of the title. Don't touch anything else.
08

Staying safe

Never paste into an AI tool or your app
  • Passwords, PINs or bank and card details
  • "API keys" or secret codes that a website gives you
  • Other people's personal information, like names, addresses or photos, without their permission
  • Anything about children that you wouldn't post publicly
  • Check before you pay. Free plans are enough to learn. Only add card details when you've decided to.
  • Be careful with "publish" or "deploy" buttons. They make your app public on the internet. That's great for sharing, as long as there's nothing private in it.
  • Trust, but check. AI can be wrong. Test what it builds, and ask: "What could go wrong with this?"
09

A gentle 4-week plan

One short session a week is plenty. Skip a week whenever life happens.

Week 1 · Warm up

Read "How to Learn This Course" (Kit 1), then build the Snake game from "Primary 1". It doesn't need to be finished.

Week 2 · Your own idea

Pick a tool from Kit 2. Build one tiny thing for your own life: a checklist, a tracker or a family page.

Week 3 · Make it better

Improve last week's build with three small changes. Read "Prompting" and "Common Pitfalls" in Kit 3.

Week 4 · Share it

Show your build to a friend or in our Discord, and come to a gathering, or bring someone new.
